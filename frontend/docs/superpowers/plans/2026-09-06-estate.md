# Estate implementation plan

**Goal:** Deliver a complete, playable local 3D property game for 2–4 players, including optional computer opponents.

**Architecture:** A seeded, pure TypeScript reducer owns the rules. React presents and dispatches actions. A Three.js scene subscribes to state and animates the physical board, dice, and pieces without deciding game outcomes.

**Tech stack:** React, TypeScript, Vite, Three.js, Lucide, Vitest, Playwright.

The user's detailed build brief is the specification and authorization for implementation. Work stays in the supplied project, with no deployment or commits required.

## Deliverables

- [x] `src/game/board.ts`, `types.ts`, `engine.ts`: 40 original spaces, seeded dice/cards, buying, rent, jail, doubles, GO, taxes, development, mortgages, trades, debt, bankruptcy, and last-player-standing victory.
- [x] `src/game/engine.test.ts`: exercise turn transitions and financial invariants; simulate full games with repeatable seeds.

- [x] `src/App.tsx`, `App.css`, `index.css`: finished dark game workspace, player status, event history, contextual action dock, local autosave, optional synthesized audio, and computer-player scheduling.
- [x] Verify engine tests, production build, lint, and browser interaction on desktop/mobile. Review the actual rendered board and the buy/turn flow.

## Rules decisions

- Default classic starting cash is $1,500; quick mode starts at $1,000. Its rent multiplier becomes 2× at round 15, 3× at round 20, and increases by 1× every five rounds to prevent economic stalemates.
- Passing on an available property leaves it in the bank (no auctions in this prototype).
- A full color group is needed to build; building/selling is even, four houses upgrade to a hotel, mortgaged properties collect no rent.
- Jail permits paying $50 or trying doubles for up to three turns. Three consecutive doubles send a player to jail.
- Trading requires the initiating player's offer and the recipient's acceptance; cash, ownership, and building restrictions are revalidated atomically.
- Required payments first offer selling buildings and mortgaging; bankruptcy transfers remaining assets to a player creditor or returns them to the bank.
- Save only stable phases so reloading cannot interrupt a financial transaction or strand an animation.
